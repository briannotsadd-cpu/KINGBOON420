## 1. Who controls permission?

| Question | Finding | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Is Wat Arun a royal temple? | Yes — first-class royal temple (Wat Arun Ratchawararam Ratchawaramahawihan), Bangkok Yai district | [S1] |
| Is the site under heritage conservation? | The Fine Arts Department (กรมศิลปากร) is tasked by the Ministry of Culture with the UNESCO nomination dossier and with conservation and site management; the central pagoda was added to UNESCO's World Heritage **Tentative List** | [S1][S2] |
| Who else? | The temple itself (abbot, as legal representative for temple affairs — doc 01 §3) controls access and activities inside the temple grounds. Whether additional royal-household or other agency approval is needed: **OPEN** | doc 01; HYPOTHESIS |
| Is there a published permit procedure for scanning/commercial imaging? | **Not found.** | open |

**Practical conclusion (HYPOTHESIS):** A capture/digitisation project would need *written* permission from the temple (abbot) **and** very likely the Fine Arts Department as the conservation authority, and the Tentative List status makes the site politically visible. Do not approach commercially without both. This matches `3D_STRATEGY.md` §5 ("written permission from the temple") but **adds the Fine Arts Department** — see proposed change in REPORT.

## 2. Drone rules (CAAT/NBTC)

As reported by Thai government tourism pages (TAT news, thailand.go.th) and a drone-law site [S3]:
- Drones cannot be legally operated until registered with **NBTC** (ownership/radio frequency; all drones regardless of weight) and with **CAAT** (drone and operator; required for drones with camera/recording devices, 2–25 kg, and above 25 kg; operator certificate via CAAT UAS portal).
- Special operations — night flights, above 90 m, entry into restricted zones — need prior written approval; flights are registered through the official CAAT app, which shows valid no-fly zones.
- Whether Wat Arun's area is a no-fly zone: **not confirmed in this run** — check in the CAAT app and with CAAT before any plan. Central Bangkok has many restrictions (HYPOTHESIS).
- Recommendation: **do not plan drone capture for the pilot.** Use ground-level public-area photography for reference only (3D_STRATEGY option 1).

## 3. Licensing landscape for existing models

- A Sketchfab account named "sukritact" was listed with a model titled "Wat Arun"; its licence was **not visible** in the results [S4]. Sketchfab hosts models under various Creative Commons licences, including CC BY and CC BY-SA (general fact seen in summaries) [S4].
- A news item reports a 3D scan done to check a temple's tilt (Bangkok Post, "3D scan to check on temple tilt") — shows that scans exist for conservation but is not evidence of reuse rights [S4].
- **Recommendation:** treat any marketplace model as unusable until the licence is read, commercial use and modification are explicit, and the author is identified (matches 3D_STRATEGY §5). CC BY-SA would force share-alike on derived work — likely incompatible with a commercial product (general knowledge, HYPOTHESIS). Never mirror or re-host scans of the temple. Add each candidate to `assets/3d/LICENSES.md` (Agent 05).
- Even a permissive licence on the *model* does not clear rights in the underlying monument imagery or conservation-authority rules: **ask the authority**.

## 4. Recommendation for `3D_STRATEGY.md` §5

1. Keep option 1 (commissioned stylized model from own public-area photos) as default; **add** a gate: "send a written enquiry to the abbot and Fine Arts Department describing a stylised, non-surveying, non-detailed representation, and record the reply" before publishing any Wat Arun-named imagery.
2. Option 2 (temple-authorised capture): require a written permit signed by the temple and confirmed by the Fine Arts Department; drone only with NBTC + CAAT registration and airspace check [S3].
3. Option 3 (licensed model): only with a written commercial licence; exclude CC BY-SA/NC.
4. Do not name Wat Arun in marketing (R-16) until consent.
5. For the pilot, **the pilot temple's own 3D need not be Wat Arun** — a friendly temple that consents removes most of this risk (D-1/D-2).
